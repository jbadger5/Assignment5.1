Many people drink coffee on a weekly or daily basis, so this amenity is important to people's lives and rountines. In this dataset I was interested in exploring the potential socio-economic impacts of whether a customer will accept a coupon to a coffee shop. So the plots I generated tried to explore the many factors that contribute to somoene's socio-economic status. After seeing the impact of a customer's bar habits on the likelihood that they will accept a bar coupon, I wanted to first see if this was also true for coffee drinkers. So for all 3 of my plots, I chose to create a bar plots that first separated the dataset into the frequency that a customer visists a coffee shop. I then split each of these groups into the smaller subgroups of gender, income, and education. While these bar plots do seem busy, I tried using a heatmap but did not think those plots properly conveyed the different porportions of accepted coffee coupons. 

In these plots, there is a clear impact of on a customer's coffee visiting habits. If a customer visits a coffee shop more than 1-3 times a month, then they are likely to accept a coffee coupon at a higher rate than the average customer. While there is minimal differences between males and females, there are subtle but real differences when the dataset is split by the customer's annual income and education level. If a customer has a lower annual income or less overall education then they are more likely to accept a coupon for a coffee. This is true across all coffee shop visitation habits. So if one were to build a model that tries to predict a customer's likelihood of accepting a coffee coupon then it would be imperative to include both a customer's annual income and education into that model.
